# SAHJONY Global Trade — Commercial Revenue Strike Doctrine

## Primary objective
Maximize legitimate collected SAHJONY revenue per unit of time and capital while deploying no SAHJONY inventory capital unless explicitly approved by the owner after the business has sufficient track record, controls, banking, insurance, and working capital.

## Core model
SAHJONY operates buyer-first and demand-first. We monetize verified commercial demand through paid sourcing engagements, retainers, documented success fees, introductions, and managed-transaction services. We do not buy speculative inventory to create a deal.

## Revenue hierarchy
1. Paid sourcing / engagement fee — fastest cash.
2. Success fee on a completed eligible transaction.
3. Recurring procurement retainer / account.
4. Managed-trade coordination fee.
5. Principal trading only after explicit owner approval and adequate capital/control infrastructure.

## Revenue Priority Score
Every prospect and opportunity should be ranked on a 0–100 operating score using evidence, not headline deal size.

### Positive factors
- +25 verified current buying requirement with product, quantity, destination, timeline, and commercial terms.
- +15 identifiable decision-maker / procurement contact.
- +15 buyer has replied or initiated the inquiry.
- +10 clear payment instrument / commercial capacity evidence suitable for diligence.
- +10 transaction can be executed without SAHJONY funding inventory.
- +10 repeat / recurring procurement potential.
- +10 real CRM intake exists with explicit target budget or quantity.
- +5 qualification completed and no critical compliance blocker.

### Negative factors
- -30 research-only lead with no buyer response or demand evidence.
- -25 ambiguous or contradictory RFQ data until clarified.
- -25 intermediary chain without verified buyer authority.
- -25 sanctions/export-control/payment-path issue until resolved.
- -20 requires SAHJONY inventory funding or deposits before monetization.
- -15 institutional onboarding with long qualification cycle and no near-term paid engagement.
- -10 unrealistic commercial terms or pricing.

## Priority bands
- A / STRIKE NOW — 75–100: immediate qualification and paid-engagement conversion.
- B / CONVERT — 55–74: active outreach and requirement capture.
- C / NURTURE — 35–54: strategic follow-up, low resource allocation.
- D / RESEARCH — 0–34: intelligence only; no substantial sourcing resources.

## Truth discipline
- Research opportunity value is not pipeline.
- Pipeline requires a real intake / explicit buyer requirement.
- Qualified pipeline requires qualification evidence.
- Active managed trade requires promotion into an actual trade case.
- Contracted value requires executed contractual evidence.
- Recognized revenue requires accounting evidence of earned/collected SAHJONY fees.
- Famous counterparties do not receive priority merely because of enterprise size.

## Daily operating order
1. Work all A-tier buyer requirements.
2. Convert A-tier requirements to paid engagements before heavy sourcing.
3. Work B-tier replies and follow-ups.
4. Source only against credible demand.
5. Escalate compliance/payment/logistics blockers immediately.
6. Disqualify or downgrade stale, vague, unverified, or capital-intensive opportunities.
7. After any successful transaction, pursue the buyer's next procurement cycle immediately.

## Current strategic lanes
- FAST CASH: fresh buyer RFQs, mid-market importers/distributors, recurring procurement needs, paid sourcing mandates.
- BUILD: Cuba private-sector sourcing where legally eligible and payment/logistics gates can be satisfied.
- STRATEGIC: IndianOil and other institutional crude counterparty onboarding; valuable track-record lane but not allowed to crowd out faster paid mandates.
- HOLD / FAIL-CLOSED: unverifiable mandates, prohibited counterparties, sanctions-evasion structures, unsupported proof-of-funds claims, speculative inventory purchases.

## Executive KPI order
1. Collected SAHJONY revenue.
2. Paid mandates signed.
3. Expected SAHJONY fee from qualified opportunities.
4. Qualified demand count.
5. Median days from first response to paid mandate.
6. Repeat buyer rate.
7. Active managed-trade cases.
8. Gross pipeline.
9. Research opportunity value.

The operating system should optimize for the top metrics, not the bottom metrics.
