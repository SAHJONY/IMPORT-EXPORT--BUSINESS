EXPORTER-FACING CUBA TERMS

Evaluate the Cuba channel before you quote.

This page explains the supplier-side information SAHJONY needs to evaluate lawful commercial opportunities involving Cuba. It is not legal advice and it does not guarantee that a product, buyer, bank or route is eligible.

What we need from a supplier

  • Legal company identity, manufacturing/export authority and responsive business contact.
  • Product description, origin, HS/technical classification where available, certifications and manufacturer evidence.
  • MOQ, current lead time, Incoterm, price basis, validity and payment terms.
  • Any export-license, end-use, territory or banking restrictions known to the supplier.

What SAHJONY reviews

  • Buyer and end-use context.
  • Product eligibility and any relevant U.S. sanctions/export-control nexus.
  • Origin, routing, banks/payment path and logistics feasibility.
  • Document requirements and whether the opportunity can move to a quote.

Non-U.S. suppliers

A Colombian, Mexican, European, Chinese or other non-U.S. supplier is not automatically outside U.S. controls. U.S.-origin content, U.S. persons, U.S. banks, dollar clearing, reexports, sanctions rules and the specific product/end user can create additional requirements.

SAHJONY will not represent a corridor as executable until the applicable commercial and compliance gates are satisfied.

Payment mechanics

There is no universal Cuba payment method. The lawful structure depends on product, parties, authorization, banks and transaction documents. Agricultural and non-agricultural transactions can be subject to different rules.

See the dedicated Cuba payments page for the current customer-facing control framework and official-source links.

Supplier identity & pricing

Supplier pricing is non-public by default. Buyer-facing disclosure, intermediary protection, customer identity, commissions and exclusivity are transaction-specific and must follow the approved commercial path.

What registration does not mean

Registration is not Cuba approval, a purchase order, a sanctions determination, a representation agreement, an exclusive territory or a promise of buyer demand.

Before quoting a Cuba opportunity, suppliers should be prepared to provide enough product, origin, end-use, payment and logistics information for the corridor to be evaluated. SAHJONY may pause or reject a transaction that cannot be structured lawfully or banked/logistically with sufficient evidence.
