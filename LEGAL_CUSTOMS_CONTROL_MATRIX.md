# SAHJONY Global Trade — Legal & Customs Control Matrix

This control matrix is operational guidance for the platform. It is not a substitute for transaction-specific advice from a licensed customs broker, attorney, tax adviser, freight forwarder, or the relevant regulator.

## Universal transaction controls

Every international trade case must evaluate and retain evidence for:

1. Counterparty identity, business purpose, beneficial ownership and authority.
2. OFAC/BIS and other applicable restricted/denied-party screening for seller, buyer, consignee, end user, freight forwarder, broker, banks and other material parties.
3. Anti-bribery/FCPA risk for agents, commissions, government touchpoints, gifts, travel and unusual payments.
4. BIS antiboycott review of contracts, letters of credit, purchase orders, certificates and shipping instructions.
5. Commodity classification: HTS for U.S. imports; Schedule B/HTS for AES as permitted; ECCN/EAR99 where U.S. export controls apply.
6. Country of origin, marking, preferential-origin and substantial-transformation analysis where applicable.
7. Customs valuation: transaction value or other lawful basis; assists, royalties, commissions, packing and related-party issues documented.
8. Incoterm plus explicit contractual allocation of title, risk, freight, insurance, export clearance, import clearance, duties and taxes.
9. Complete commercial paper trail: quote/proforma, PO, contract, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, proof of payment and relevant correspondence.
10. Cargo insurance and trade-credit insurance applicability.
11. Claims procedure for loss, damage, shortage, delay, detention/demurrage, customs detention and rejected goods.
12. Regulatory record retention and audit trail.

## U.S. import controls

- Confirm importer of record and importer number.
- Confirm licensed customs broker/POA where used; the importer retains compliance responsibility.
- Determine bond requirement and sufficiency.
- Determine entry type, cargo release, entry summary and duty/fee deposit requirements.
- Determine HTS classification, customs value, country of origin and marking.
- Determine antidumping/countervailing duty, Section 201/232/301 or other trade-remedy applicability where relevant.
- Determine quota, special tariff program and preference claims.
- Screen admissibility and prohibited/restricted merchandise.
- Perform forced-labor/UFLPA supply-chain traceability review where sourcing creates risk.
- Determine Partner Government Agency requirements. Product triggers may include FDA, USDA/APHIS/FSIS, EPA, FCC, CPSC, DOT/NHTSA/PHMSA, FTC, TTB, DEA, FWS and others.
- Do not release customer-facing shipment status as customs-cleared until CBP/broker evidence supports release.

## U.S. export controls

- Determine whether the item/activity is subject to the EAR.
- Determine ECCN or EAR99 classification.
- Determine destination controls, end-user/end-use restrictions and diversion risk.
- Determine license requirement, license exception or NLR and retain the legal basis.
- Screen all transaction parties and denied persons.
- Determine AES/EEI requirement and obtain/retain ITN when filing is required.
- Preserve license conditions, destination-control requirements and supporting documents.
- Review antiboycott requests and reporting obligations.

## Product-agency triggers

The platform must treat product-agency compliance as dynamic, based on product, use, composition, origin, destination and shipment method. Examples:

- Food, beverages, dietary ingredients and animal food: FDA registration/prior notice/FSVP and related requirements as applicable.
- Plants, seeds, wood, agricultural commodities, soil: USDA APHIS ACIR, permits, treatments and phytosanitary requirements as applicable.
- Animal products/byproducts: APHIS Veterinary Services and potentially USDA FSIS.
- Chemicals/pesticides: EPA TSCA/FIFRA controls as applicable.
- Radio/electronic devices: FCC authorization/import conditions as applicable.
- Consumer products: CPSC safety/certification rules as applicable.
- Vehicles/vehicle equipment: DOT/NHTSA and EPA requirements as applicable.
- Alcohol/tobacco: TTB and other federal/state requirements.
- Wildlife/protected species: U.S. Fish & Wildlife/CITES requirements.

A generic `PGA complete` checkbox is not sufficient. The applicable agency, filing/permit/certificate reference and evidence document must be captured.

## Release governance

A trade case starts `blocked`. Open, in-progress or blocked requirements keep release blocked unless a requirement is formally determined not applicable or lawfully waived with evidence. Employees may prepare and update evidence. Customers have read-only access to customer-visible status. Final platform compliance release is owner-only.

No platform determination should claim that a regulator, customs authority or legal counsel has approved a transaction unless the system stores authoritative evidence of that approval.

## Retention

The platform defaults to long-term retention and must preserve immutable audit events. CBP generally requires many import records for five years, with exceptions depending on record type. EAR/Census and product-agency retention periods may differ, so the stricter applicable rule should govern the transaction record.
